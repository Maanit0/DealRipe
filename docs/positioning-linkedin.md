# LinkedIn copy: personal profile and DealRipe company page

Written 2026-08-31, for the ICP Brian Krause pointed at: CROs and VPs of Sales
who own a number and inspect deals by hand. Same spine as dealripe.com so a
reader who clicks through hears one voice.

House rules applied throughout: no em-dashes or en-dashes, no sycophancy, no
claim the product cannot stand behind. Security and governance are framed as
capability rather than as disclaimer: the limits are real, but a storefront is
not the place to list your own gaps. Save that for the room, where a CRO who
probes gets a straight answer and it lands as candor instead of as doubt.

## PART 2: DEALRIPE COMPANY PAGE

Brian's point: sales people are the easiest audience on LinkedIn to build a
following with, 100% of them are on it, and a following takes time to build, so
the page needs to exist before you need it. Content comes after the page, not
before.

### 2.1 Creating it

Go to linkedin.com/company/setup/new and choose **Company** (not Showcase or
Educational). You need to be signed in as yourself; the page attaches to your
profile as an admin.

LinkedIn requires the name, URL, website, industry, size, type and logo before
it will let you publish. Have the logo ready first or you will get stopped
halfway.

### 2.2 Every field, filled in

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Name | DealRipe |
| LinkedIn public URL | linkedin.com/company/dealripe |
| Website | https://dealripe.com |
| Industry | Software Development |
| Company size | 2-10 employees |
| Company type | Privately Held |
| Location | San Francisco Bay Area, California, US |
| Founded | 2026 |
| Custom button | **Visit website**, pointing at https://dealripe.com |

On the custom button: use Visit website rather than Contact us. Contact us
sends people into a LinkedIn form you will check rarely. Visit website sends
them to the page that does the selling.

### 2.3 Images

| Asset | Exact size | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Logo | 300 x 300 px, PNG | Square. Shown as a circle-cropped thumbnail in feeds, so keep the mark well inside the edges. |
| Cover image | 1128 x 191 px, PNG | Very wide and short. One line of text at most, or no text. |

Both are produced as part of the brand asset set. Do not upload a screenshot or
a cropped photo here: a wrong-sized logo on a company page is the single most
common thing that makes an early-stage company look early-stage.

### 2.5 About (2,000 character limit)

Kept short on purpose. LinkedIn truncates this after a couple of lines, and the
page's job is to earn a follow and a click through to the site, not to close
anyone. Explaining everything here removes the reason to leave.

```
DealRipe puts an AI agent on every open opportunity in your pipeline, tuned to
your team's winning sales motion. It listens to all customer emails, calls, and
calendar activity, learns what your best reps do, then distributes it to the
rest of your reps on their active deals. The result: your best rep on every deal
and an up-to-date forecast grounded in customer truth, not rep interpretation.

Live in production on real deals under a paid contract. A CRO runs his weekly
pipeline review out of DealRipe rather than his CRM.

dealripe.com
```

One open flag on the wording, raised once and left to you: **"listens to all
customer emails."** The capability is fine; the phrasing is what a CRO's IT
reviewer reads first, and "all" implies the whole mailbox rather than the deal.
The scoped alternative is "reads the calls, email and calendar activity on each
deal." Your actual posture is scoped read access, no send permission and
recordings deleted after transcription, so the careful version is also the
accurate one.

The last two blocks are not decoration. On a page with no followers and no
customer logos yet, the paid-production line is the whole difference between
reading as a company and reading as a concept, and the link is the only action
the page can ask for.

### 2.7 The first week, in order

1. **Publish the page** with logo, cover, tagline, About and specialties all
   filled. A half-finished page is worse than no page.
2. **Add it to your own Experience entry.** Your DealRipe role currently says
   "Self-employed." Relink it to the page so the logo shows on your profile and
   your 862 followers get an easy path to follow it. How:

   - Profile, then the pencil on the DealRipe entry in Experience.
   - In **Company name**, delete the text and retype "DealRipe". A dropdown of
     matching company pages appears.
   - **Click the page in the dropdown.** This is the step people miss. Typing
     the name and moving on leaves it as free text and nothing links. The fig
     logo appearing in the field is how you know it took.
   - Change **Employment type** from Self-employed to Full-time.
   - Save. The entry should now show the logo and the company name should be a
     link.

   A brand-new page can take minutes to hours to appear in that dropdown. If it
   is not there immediately, wait and retry rather than retyping it as text.
3. **Invite connections.** LinkedIn gives page admins a monthly allowance of
   invitations. Spend the first batch on sales leaders rather than on everyone.
4. **Ask Brian to follow and reshare it once.** Ten seconds of his time is worth
   more than a month of posting into an empty room.
5. **Then, and only then, start publishing.** Brian's sequence was page first,
   followers second, content third. Content into a page with nine followers is
   effort with no distribution.
